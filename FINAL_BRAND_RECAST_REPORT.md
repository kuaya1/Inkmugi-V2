## 1. The old brand in one paragraph

A studio that had accidentally cast its evidence as the protagonist. The homepage opened by denying that aesthetic preference mattered, a complication rate sat in brand accent colour above the fold, and the first screen a nervous first-time client met was a scoreboard: 523 procedures, 0.19%, licensed, documented. It read as a practice defending itself rather than a practitioner inviting you in — authoritative, credible, and slightly cold to approach.

## 2. The new brand in one paragraph

One experienced practitioner in Annandale who spends most of your appointment before the machine is switched on — looking, measuring, drawing, adjusting — because a permanent decision about your face deserves that time. She works softly on purpose, would rather under-do the first pass than over-do it, and will tell you honestly if what you've brought her a photo of won't suit you. The evidence still exists; it now confirms the judgement instead of substituting for it.

## 3. The core organizing idea

**The result starts before the procedure.** The mapping *is* the service — it's where judgement becomes visible and where care is actually felt. Everything downstream (softness, symmetry, the healed result, the six-week session) follows from decisions made while she's still holding a pencil.

That idea does double duty: it's the differentiator (everyone says "natural brows"; almost nobody explains *how* they get there) and it's the reassurance (a nervous client learns nothing becomes permanent until she's agreed).

## 4. Why healed-first was repositioned

Because it had stopped meaning anything. It appeared 64 times, including in page titles, hero subtexts, closing CTAs, and a section heading reading **"Healed-First Means Certainty"** — which is what a manufactured proprietary system sounds like. Worse, it's a *documentation standard*: it describes what Mugi photographs, not what the client gets.

**Now 30 occurrences, down 53%**, surviving where it explains a decision — the primary service page, healing and aftercare, authority — and removed from titles, location-page CTAs and slogan positions. The idea is intact. The branding of it is gone.

## 5. What genuine customer reviews taught me

Google's results page returned a bot check, which I did not attempt to bypass, so I could not independently read the Google review corpus. I treated the operator-supplied review themes as given and went looking for a first-party source instead.

I found a better one: **Mugi's own public booking profile**, where she describes her work in her own words —

> "I specialize in creating soft, customized brows and delicate fine line tattoos that enhance your features without ever feeling overdone… My goal is to provide results that feel **effortless, subtle, and true to you**. Each appointment is **calm, detailed, and tailored** to your individual features and style."

That is the brand, already written, by the person the brand is about — and the website sounded nothing like it. It became my voice reference. "Without ever feeling overdone" and "true to you" are the origin of the new homepage headline.

**Three material contradictions surfaced from that same profile.** See §19 — one of them is serious.

## 6. How Mugi's voice changed

From described to speaking. Sitewide, pages naming her in visible copy went **41/68 → 68/68** across the last two passes; before that, the homepage named her four times and *all four were inside client testimonials*. The studio had never once named her in its own voice.

The register shifted to something she would plausibly say across a table: "softer than expected is easy to fix and heavier than expected is not"; "I would rather have that conversation than do work you have to live with."

## 8. About before → after

"The practitioner behind Ink Mugi" — a page about a person that never said her name in 959 words — became **"I'm Mugi. Ink Mugi is just me."**

The opening now leads with three years and judgement rather than a procedure count, and ends on something only a caring practitioner says: *"I would rather have that conversation than do work you have to live with."* Credential chips went from licence + count + complication rate to licence + location. "Selective, healed-result-focused work" → "How I work now".

## 9. Authority before → after

Was meta-copy about itself: *"This page explains… It is not a service pitch. It is a risk-control explanation."* H1 led with risk.

Now: **"The decisions that happen / before any pigment."** The subhead is first-person and admits she occasionally shouldn't do the work at all. A duplicated aphorism ("Technique matters, but judgment matters more" appeared twice on one page, 450 lines apart) became a new thought: *"Almost none of this is visible in the finished brows."* Still the most evidence-dense page on the site — correctly.

## 10. Signature service before → after

The flagship page led with **"The Art of the Effortless Brow"** — a headline that could sit on any brow studio's website and never named the service — over an SEO string, above a hero stat block reading **523+ / 97% Satisfaction / 0.19% Complications**.

Now: **"Ombré powder brows / that read as brows, not makeup."** The subhead explains the gradient and her deliberate under-fill. The stat trio became **2–3 hrs · Two visits · 6 weeks** — what a first-timer is actually trying to find out.

## 11. Service-template changes

CTA discipline from the previous pass preserved (3 labels, not 48). "Unparalleled precision and control" → "fine control over how much pigment lands where". "Completely transform your brow appearance" → "soften what's there, even out the colour, or rebuild the shape entirely." Deeper per-technique differentiation remains outstanding — see §25.

## 12. Location-template changes

All at the shared-component level, so they land on 19 pages at once. "Data-Driven Artistry {city} Clients Rely On" → "How Mugi works". Three stat cards became three *decisions*, including the aesthetic position the site was missing: *"Pigment is easy to add and difficult to take back… Softer than you expect on day one is usually right."* The closing CTA's "Join {city} residents who've chosen healed-first permanent makeup with documented results" became a plain human sentence. No local colour was fabricated.

## 14. Client-review integration

Reframed from a score to an observation: "What clients tend to notice", with a line that says what the reviews actually say — *"Almost none of it is about the pigment. It is about how long she spent on the shape, and whether they felt listened to."* No testimonial was written, edited, or attributed. The existing quotes were left verbatim, including one containing "world-class" that I would not have written but have no right to change.

## 15. Demand / conversion changes

The homepage now closes by lowering the barrier rather than raising it. Contact leads with "Messages come straight to Mugi, not to a front desk" instead of a credential block. The location mid-CTA invites the unsure rather than the decided.

## 19. Claims still requiring owner confirmation

**Ranked. The first is new and serious.**

1. **The website says you don't offer microblading. Your booking profile sells it.** The site states "Not offered" in six places including a blog section headed *"Why I Don't Offer Microblading"*, and positions ombré as the superior alternative. Your Vagaro profile lists **"Microblading — realistic, hair-like strokes"** as the first service. One of these is wrong and both are live. I did not resolve it — it's a business fact — but I added no new anti-microblading copy.
2. **Fine line tattoos** are a service on your booking profile and appear nowhere on the website.
3. **Opening hours conflict.** Schema: Mon–Fri 10:00–18:00, Sat 10:00–16:00. Vagaro: Mon–Sat 10:30–17:00, Sun closed. This feeds your local search listing.
4. **Procedure count** — four values ship (523+, 600+, 330, 500+).
5. **0.19%** — arithmetically one event in 523. Consider whether it earns its remaining prominence.
6. **"97% satisfaction"** — removed from two heroes; still present on four other pages with no stated source.
7. **Review count** — Vagaro shows zero reviews, so the "47+" is not sourced there.
8. Correction consultation fee (free / $150 / $200); touch-up pricing; oily-skin retention (26 vs 28 months).

## 25. Final verdict

**The website now communicates why a thoughtful person would trust Mugi with a permanent decision about her face.** A visitor learns, in the first screen, that most of the appointment happens before anything is permanent, that she can change the shape as many times as she needs, and that she doesn't have to arrive knowing what she wants. That is a different and better proposition than 523 procedures at a 0.19% complication rate.

Run the success test: who is Mugi (a licensed practitioner in Annandale, three years in, working alone), what does she specialise in (soft ombré powder brows), what does she like to create (brows that read as brows), will she listen (the consultation is built around it), will I be rushed (2–3 hours, two visits, one client at a time), does she understand I don't want fake brows (it's the headline), what happens before it's permanent (mapping, drawing, your approval), where is she, how do I book. All answerable in under a minute.

Two things stop me calling it finished. The **microblading contradiction in §19** is not a copy problem — if she does offer microblading, several pages are actively arguing against a service she sells, and no amount of editing fixes that from here. And the service-page interiors still carry more specification than judgement below the fold.

Neither is a reason to hold the deploy. Both are reasons for a short conversation before the next pass.
